## Scene 7 — "An agent with tools" (100.6–109.4)

**Visual.** Paper ground, same look as the film. The planner window and the customer window from scene 6 shrink into the centre and **become reo** (object becomes the world): reo (sunset gradient, no vest) is left floating in the middle, slowly bobbing (a 3–4 px breathing hover, an outBack arrival), eyes looking at each tile as it lands.

Around reo, **eight tool tiles** arrive one by one on a loose ring (radius ~380 px, not a perfect circle), each connected to reo by a thin line that draws in (the only "connection animation" in the film, and it is the point here). Order and wording:

| Tile | Label | Status on screen | Honest status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brain / engine | "Engine" | solid | **real**: every size and price comes from the engine |
| Calendar | "Calendar" | solid | **shown in the demo**: it proposes the site-visit slot; the demo books it in a mock calendar |
| CRM | "CRM" | solid | **shown in the demo**: it proposes the follow-up task; mock CRM |
| Satellite | "Roof photo" | solid | **real**: roof traced on the photo, modules placed |
| WhatsApp | "WhatsApp" | outlined, labelled *next* | **vision** (not built) |
| Email | "Email" | outlined, *next* | **vision** |
| Documents | "Documents" | outlined, *next* | **vision** (bill reading exists, the rest does not) |
| ERP / pricing | "Price lists" | outlined, *next* | **vision** |

Solid tiles = what the audience just watched. Outlined tiles with a small "next" mark = vision. This keeps the claim honest while still showing the ambition; if you would rather everything look solid, say so, but then the voice-over must say "vision" for all of it.

**Icons.** Drawn by us as simple glyphs in each tool's recognisable colour (WhatsApp green bubble with a handset, a calendar page, a CRM node graph, a brain made of the Reonic blades). Official logo files are better, but they would be downloaded assets with their own licences: if you supply them I will drop them in.

## Scene 8 — "What it is worth" (109.4–115.4)

**Visual.** The tiles fall away; reo steps left and shrinks. One large figure on the right in Geist 700, with a one-line method under it and a small "conservative" tag. No chart: a single number the judges can repeat.

**The number, from the figures you found (all third-party or company-claimed, so the slide says so):**

| Input | Value | Source on the slide |
|---|---|---|
| Reonic ARR | about **$20M** | "reported by arr.club, unverified" |
| Reonic customers | **3,000+** installers | "company-claimed" |
| Adoption (our assumption) | **10 %** = 300 installers | "conservative case" |
| reo add-on price (our assumption) | **$199 per installer per month** | "assumed" |

300 × $199 × 12 = **$716,400**, about **$0.7M of new ARR, +3.6 % on the reported $20M**. A 5 % case ($0.36M, +1.8 %) is the floor if you want it even more conservative; both are far below what the reported growth (about 20× in two years) would suggest, which is the point of "conservative".

**On screen:** the big figure **+$0.7M ARR** (Geist 700, ~220 px) with "+3.6 %" in small type next to it, and under it the method in one line: "10 % of 3,000+ installers × $199 a month". A 12 px source line at the bottom: "Reonic ARR ≈ $20M (arr.club, third-party) · 3,000+ customers (company-claimed) · price and adoption are our assumptions". The source line stays on screen the whole beat; the film should never present the reported numbers as verified.
